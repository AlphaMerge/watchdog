THE FIRST RUN THAT SKIPPED NOTHING.

Every knob set, both workspaces reporting `skipped 0`, exit 0:

  MS_BENCH_MINUTES=60  MS_SOAK_MINUTES=60  MS_SOAK_FRACTION=1
  MS_BENCH_SWEEP=1     MS_PING_SWEEP=1     MS_BENCH_FILL=1

14:34:40 to 17:00:23 local, 2 h 25 m. Conditions recorded by the run itself
(P-192): load 0.23 per CPU, delivered CPU 1.03x the reference machine, nothing
else on the shared PostgreSQL. `ms-server` and the Vite container stopped
throughout, and left stopped when it was suggested they come back up mid-run --
which is what cost the P-15 figure on 2026-09-08's earlier attempt.

An ordinary `test:perf` measures scaled-down proxies of three criteria and
skips three more cases outright. This is what the real ones say.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE ARCHITECTURE GATE REPRODUCES
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

P-15's sustained hour, against what PLAN.md has recorded since 2026-08-26:

  checks dispatched       300,095       300,000

  refused                 0             0

             (wheel p95 7 ms, wait p95 202 ms), rss +619 MB at 64 workers

Two years of scheduling in one hour, and the wheel contributes 7 ms of the
206. The rest is waiting for a worker, which is the pool's business and what
the sweep below is about.

3. THE POOL SWEEP SAYS WHY THE DEFAULT IS 64
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

  pool     jitter p95     rss
     7       15,890 ms    +41 MB
    16        3,175 ms   +117 MB
    64          206 ms   +619 MB

Two orders of magnitude across the curve, and it is why `P-30` called the
shipped default wrong by a factor of sixteen.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE ICMP SWEEP ANSWERS THE QUESTION P-15 RAISED
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

`P-15` met an ICMP shortfall by asking for a 250-slot pool. The curve says
whether that was necessary or merely one way out -- every slot costs a worker
and about 6 MB:

  in flight   default   with -i 0.2   gain
     16        5/s        25/s        4.9x
     32       10/s        50/s        4.9x
     64       20/s        98/s        4.9x
    128       40/s       179/s        4.5x
    256       75/s       310/s        4.1x

The interval flag buys ~4.9x and costs nothing; going 64 -> 256 in flight buys
3.75x and costs 192 workers. `P-07` shipped the flag, and this is the
measurement that says it was the better lever.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. AND ONE RESULT THAT IS ABOUT THE SUITE, NOT THE CODE
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

  serial       29.7 s   [29.7, 32.2, 27.8]   spread 4.4 s    1% spare
  8 in flight   8.0 s   [ 8.0, 11.5,  7.1]   spread 4.4 s   73% spare
  bulk          2.4 s   [ 2.3,  2.4,  2.5]   spread 0.2 s   92% spare

The serial build passed on its median and **one of its three samples went over
the 30 s budget**. The same measurement read 20.9 to 25.7 s across four runs
earlier the same day, on the same commit.

What differs is what ran before it. This run put the replay soak at 100% and an
hour of heavy writing through the same PostgreSQL ahead of the tree build.
`P-193` found this measurement was not independent of the other cases in its
own file; this says it is not independent of the other files either.

**The two hour-long soaks in the same run landing exactly on their records is
the evidence that this is ordering and not a regression.** A machine slow
enough to add 5 s to the tree build would not have returned p95 206 ms against
a recorded 216.

Bulk moved 0.2 s across the same three samples. As on 2026-09-08's earlier
runs, the noise is proportional to the round-trips, and only the serial case
has many.

The fill sweep replicated `P-194` cleanly -- no fill effect, no trend either
direction, 24 to 29 s at every level:

  already there     bulk up  bulk down  serial up  serial down
  0 objects           2.3 s      2.4 s     24.3 s       26.9 s
  5,000 objects       6.9 s      7.0 s     28.4 s       29.4 s
  10,000 objects      1.7 s      2.7 s     24.1 s       24.3 s
  20,000 objects      1.7 s      1.8 s     25.3 s       24.2 s

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT TO DO WITH IT
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Nothing to a budget. The gate reproduces, the soaks are clean, and the two
sweeps confirm the two defaults they were taken to choose.

The tree build's 1% margin here is a measurement artefact of running it after
two hours of database work, and reading it as anything else would be the third
time in one day that someone drew a conclusion about the code from a number
that was about the harness. What it does say is that this case cannot be
compared across runs with different suites in front of it -- which the test now
prints in its own output, and which is worth one line in any future write-up
that quotes it.
