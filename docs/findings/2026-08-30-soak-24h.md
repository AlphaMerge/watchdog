# Measurements: 24-hour soak at 5,000 sensors (H-01)

- **Date**: 2026-08-29 14:51 UTC → 2026-08-30 14:51 UTC, 23.99 h, 1,439 samples at one a minute
- **Load**: 5,047 sensors on the probe's wheel (5,000 `port.tcp` provisioned for this run against
  a local listener, plus the 47 the dev tree already carried), 60-second interval
- **Measured on**: the dev stack — core and probe in one container, PostgreSQL 17 with
  TimescaleDB in another, on an 8-core arm64 machine
- **Raw data**: `soak-20260829T145105Z.jsonl` (samples, one JSON object each) and
  `soak-20260829T145105Z-rollups.txt` (225 rollup verification runs)
- **Reproduce**: `scripts/soak.sh`, or `scripts/soak/sample.mjs` against a provisioned tree, then
  `node scripts/soak/report.mjs <file>`

## The three things `PLAN.md` asked for

> 24-hour soak at 5,000 sensors: no memory growth, no lag drift, correct rollups throughout.

All three hold.

| Series | mean | slope | budget | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| core RSS | 310.0 MB | **+1.10 MB/h** | 3.10 | pass |
| core heap | 82.1 MB | **+0.08 MB/h** | 0.82 | pass |
| probe RSS | 1,790.4 MB | **+0.68 MB/h** | 17.90 | pass |
| scheduler lag p95 | 10.7 ms | **−0.02 ms/h** | 41.22 | pass |
| ingestion queue | 24.8 readings | +0.07/h | 1,665 | pass |
| rollup lag | 78.0 s | +0.25/h | 146.75 | pass |
| pool in use | 1.3 % | −0.04/h | 3.28 | pass |
| checks waiting | 0.08 | +0.003/h | — | pass, see below |

**Memory is flat.** Probe RSS moved 0.68 MB an hour on a 1,790 MB footprint — 16 MB across the
day, a quarter of one percent. The core moved 1.1 MB an hour on 310 MB. Neither is growth; both
are the sawtooth a garbage-collected heap makes at a steady workload.

**Scheduler lag did not drift.** A p95 of 10.7 ms against `P-15`'s budget of 1,000 ms, with a
slope of −0.02 ms/h. The pool sat at 1.3% in use with a queue that was empty in 1,434 of 1,439
samples.

**Rollups were correct throughout.** 225 verification runs at 15-minute intervals, each comparing
every rollup bucket against a recomputation from the raw samples underneath it: **13,694,541
bucket comparisons, zero disagreements** — no wrong count, sum, min or max, and no missing
bucket. 7,544,624 readings were written during the 24 hours, about 87 a second.

## What the run cost to trust

**Three of the eight series first reported FAIL, and none of the three was a fault.** They were
mistakes in the report, and they are worth recording because each is a way to make a soak
worthless.

- **The check compared the absolute slope**, so an ingestion queue *draining* from 30 to 5 was
  reported as drift. Only growth is a finding; every check is one-sided now.
- **It judged rollup lag against 1% of its own mean**, calling +4.4 s/h a failure on a run whose
  lag never exceeded 226 s against a declared warning threshold of 3,600 — forty times stricter
  than the product's own definition of bad. Where a channel declares limits (`S-18`) those are
  now the yardstick, and the question is the one an operator actually has: at this slope, is the
  channel still below its declared warning bound after twenty-four hours?
- **`checks waiting` has a mean of 0.08**, so a proportional budget came out at 0.001/h — a
  threshold no real measurement clears. It was above zero in five samples out of 1,439 and spent
  its entire last quarter at exactly zero, yet a line fitted through two early spikes sloped up.
  A series that ended no higher than it started is not drifting upward, so both signals must now
  agree before anything fails.

Each relaxation was checked against a synthetic run rather than assumed: one leaking 60 MB/h
still fails on core RSS, and one whose rollup lag climbs 200 s/h still fails against the 130 s/h
its own mean leaves it. A soak that cries wolf on a healthy run is worse than no soak, because
the next person reads past it — and a soak relaxed until nothing fails is worse still.

**The harness could not say what it had measured.** Every sample recorded `sensorCount: null`,
because the sampler asked `/objects?kind=sensor&limit=1` for a `total` that endpoint does not
have — it is keyset-paginated and answers `{items, nextCursor}`. The load was in the data all
along as the probe's `scheduled` channel; the report reads that now. A record of a measurement
that cannot say what it measured is most of the way to not being a record.

## What the soak found

**One defect, and it was found before the run started** — while proving the harness could see a
stalled spool at all.

`SpoolDrain` advanced its cursor past the last result of a segment and then asked for more from
that position. `peek` returned empty for ever, the drain concluded there was nothing to send, and
the link stayed up while results piled up behind a cursor parked on a boundary. It needed a
segment roll *after* a catch-up, so it did not fire on a probe that had never fallen behind — but
once it fired it never recovered, and nothing said so: no error, no state change, a healthy link
and a rising spool. 30,000 results accumulated in the reproduction before anything would have
noticed, and only the `spoolDropped` cap would eventually have reported it, long after the data
was gone. Fixed in `615c9f3`; `92ec56a` added a stall detector so a drain that makes no progress
with results waiting says so.

**Every test in `WORKFLOW.MD §13` row 3 passed throughout**, which is the uncomfortable part.
`spool-drain.test.ts` and `replay-pacer.test.ts` both drain a spool and both were green, because
neither drains, rolls a segment, and drains again. `docs/findings/2026-08-29-failure-modes.md`
now records that.

**And one that only a soak could have shown: `P-26`.** The check pool grew to 48 workers in 20
events during the provisioning burst and then shrank **zero** times in 24 hours while `poolUsage`
sat between 0% and 3% — forty-five workers spare at all times, none returned. `#considerShrink`
resets its idle clock on every `submit`, so "idle" means "no check has arrived at all for a
minute", and a probe watching 5,000 sensors submits about eighty-three a second. The window never
opens. The policy is inert precisely on the probe that grew a large pool.

That is also the whole of the probe's 1,790 MB: 48 worker threads, each a V8 isolate that loads
the sensor catalogue. `P-18` measured 557 MB for 48 workers *at start*; a worker that has run
checks for an hour holds more. The footprint is flat, so it is headroom rather than a leak — but
it is set by the worst burst the probe ever saw, which is a poor thing to have to tell an
operator, and is what `P-26` exists to fix.

## Caveats

- **This machine also ran ten test suites during the first 75 minutes of steady state** — eight of
  `@ms/sensors` and two full parallel `npm test` runs — for `P-25`. Real CPU contention on the
  same host as the core and the probe. It is annotated in the raw data as a `note` record.
- **One host, one probe, one database, all local.** Nothing here measures a remote probe, a
  network partition, or a database on another machine. `WORKFLOW.MD §13` covers those by test
  rather than by soak.
- **`port.tcp` against a local listener is a cheap check.** It exercises the scheduler, the pool,
  the wire, ingestion, rollups and retention at full rate, which is what this was for. It does not
  exercise SNMP, SSH or WMI transports under load.
- The dev stack is not a production install: no systemd, and the core and probe share a container.

## What this unblocks

`H-08` (published sizing guidance) needs numbers per hardware tier and now has one tier measured.
The storage side was already measured in `2026-08-25-timeseries-sizing.md`; this adds the
CPU-and-memory side for a single 8-core arm64 host at the v1 target.
